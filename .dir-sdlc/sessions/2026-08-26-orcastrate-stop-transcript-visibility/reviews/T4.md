### Blocking finding

**The new obligation comment asserts something untrue of the shipped code** — `tests/unit/store/migration-frozen.test.ts:51-54`: *"Nothing in this file recorded the obligation when migration 4 landed, so migration 4 shipped unpinned too."*

Migration 4 did **not** ship unpinned. `e15b1c4:src/main/store/schema.ts:291` declares `MIGRATION_4_LANE_STATUSES` and line 605 is the only `sqlIn` in `MIGRATION_4`'s body — migration 4 is the one migration that shipped fully pinned. *(Independently verified by the orchestrator before dispatching the revision.)*

The falsehood is load-bearing twice: it is the stated consequence ("so …") and the stated reason the paragraph exists ("so the third time does not happen"). The very next paragraph defines "pin" as pinning the interpolated list as a migration-local constant — the sense under which the claim is simply wrong. Migration 5's author reads this and concludes migration 4 left the same latent bug in place, the inverse of the truth.

Remediation options, best fit first:

1. **Narrow the claim to the tripwire, which is what is actually true** — e.g. *"…so migration 4 shipped without an entry here: its list was correctly pinned in `schema.ts`, but nothing was watching that it stayed that way."* One clause; keeps the argument intact and strengthens it, since the point is that pinning and watching are separate obligations. Evidence: `repo` — `e15b1c4:src/main/store/schema.ts:291,605`, and the file's own definition of "pin".
2. **Delete the "so migration 4 shipped unpinned too" clause**, keeping the preceding sentence. Removes the falsehood at minimum cost but loses the concrete instance; criterion 2 wants the argument, not a bare rule. Evidence: `repo`.
3. **Recast the paragraph around migration 1 as the sole cited instance.** Safe but weaker — migration 4 is the case that motivated the task. Evidence: `judgment`.
